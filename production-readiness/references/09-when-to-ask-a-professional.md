# 9. When to ask a lawyer or another professional

Last verified: 2026-11-02. The agent and this repository are **not** a substitute for professional advice. This file protects the developer and the site owner by listing when a human expert is the right next step, and how to make that consultation cheap and useful.

## Ask a lawyer (in the owner's jurisdiction) when…

- **Before public launch**, always, for the privacy policy, terms and cookie policy. A one-hour consultation reviewing a well-prepared draft costs far less than writing from scratch.
- Your audience includes **minors**, or the content is for teens (consent, guardian authorisation, retention).
- You process **sensitive data** (health, beliefs, orientation, biometrics, precise location, children's data).
- You **sell** directly, take payments, run subscriptions, or offer prizes/contests.
- You **transfer data abroad** (most cloud hosting does) and the law of your country restricts it.
- A law may require **registering** your database or appointing someone with the data authority (check, do not assume).
- You publish **user content or reviews**, use **affiliate links**, or run sponsored posts (advertising and endorsement rules).
- You use others' **images, fonts, text or music** and licences are unclear.
- There is a **data breach** or a deletion/access request you do not know how to answer.
- You sign anything with a client: **who owns the code, the content, the domain and the data**, who is liable, and what the developer's maintenance duties are.

## Ask a security professional when…

- You handle payments, health or identity data, or are a target of regulation.
- Before a launch with significant traffic: an independent review or penetration test.
- After an incident.
- Automated reviews (including agent-run ones) find nothing: that is a floor, not a guarantee.

## Ask an accountant or business adviser when…

The site earns money (sales, ads, affiliate), or the owner needs to register a business, invoice or handle taxes.

## Protect yourself as a freelance developer

- Put in writing, even in a short agreement: the **owner is the data controller**, you act on their instructions, who pays for accounts and domains, who holds admin access, what is out of scope (legal texts, content), and that **texts you draft are drafts, not legal advice**.
- Keep the domain, hosting, email and payment accounts in the **owner's name**; you hold delegated access.
- Do not promise "GDPR/CCPA/LGPD compliant". Promise a documented, reviewed-by-a-professional process.
- Keep your own records: what you recommended, what the owner decided, when.

## Make the consultation cheap

Bring one page: data inventory (fields, purpose, retention, recipients); provider table; the age/consent flow; the draft texts with every `[PLACEHOLDER]` and `[PROPOSAL]` listed; the jurisdiction assumption; and the open questions you could not verify. Ask specific questions ("Do we have to register this database? Is hosting abroad a transfer? Is guardian authorisation by checkbox enough for 16–17?").

## Cheaper routes (verify availability)

A short paid consultation with a data-protection or digital-law lawyer; the data-protection authority's published guides or enquiry channel; the owner's professional association; a trusted contact with legal training for a read-through. None replaces a review of the final texts.
